How to save your grandparents’ voice and stories

Photos show how someone looked. Their voice reminds you who they were. Recording it takes fifteen minutes and a phone — and it is one of the things families most often wish they had done sooner.

1. Use the phone you already have

A normal phone voice recorder or a WhatsApp voice note is enough. Sit close — about an arm’s length — in a quiet room with the fan and TV off. Soft furnishings (a sofa, curtains) make the sound warmer.

2. Ask for stories, not facts

People speak most naturally when they remember a scene. Try:

  • “What was your house like when you were a child?”
  • “How did you and Grandpa meet?”
  • “What was the hardest year of your life, and how did you get through it?”
  • “What was your mother like?”
  • “What do you want your great-grandchildren to know?”
💡 Ask them to sing a song or say a blessing in their own language. Those few seconds become the most precious part of the recording.

3. Keep it short and come back

Ten or fifteen minutes at a time is plenty. Elders tire quickly, and the best stories come out on the second or third visit, once they know you are really listening.

4. Write down who, when and where

Name every file clearly — “Grandmother — childhood in Amritsar — 2026”. Without a name, a recording on an old phone is easily lost.

5. Keep more than one copy

Phones get lost and chats get deleted. Save the recordings in at least two places, and attach them to the person in your family tree so everyone can find them.

6. Let the story be heard again

With Royal or a Voice Story Pack, a short voice sample lets DynastyVault read a written life story aloud in a voice that sounds like theirs — so grandchildren can hear the story “told by” the person it is about. If you’d rather not do it yourself, we can make it for you.
