How to make a family tree — both sides, step by step

You don’t need records, a library or a weekend. Most families can draw three or four generations in one evening — just by asking the right people the right questions.

1. Start with yourself and go up

Write your own name first. Above it, your father and mother. Above them, both sets of grandparents. This is the “core” of every family tree, and you probably already know most of these names.

💡 Write full names, including surnames before marriage. A grandmother’s maiden name is often the key that opens up a whole second family.

2. Do both sides — not just one

Many family trees only follow the father’s side. But half of your story comes from your mother’s family. Keep the two sides next to each other: paternal grandparents on the left, maternal grandparents on the right, meeting at your parents and you.

3. Ask the elders this week

The best source is still a grandparent, an aunt or an old uncle. Call them, or ask at the next family lunch. Useful questions:

  • What were your parents’ and grandparents’ names?
  • Where were you born, and where did the family live before that?
  • Who moved, when, and why?
  • How many brothers and sisters did you have? Who did they marry?
  • What did people in the family do for work?
🎙️ Record the conversation on your phone. Years later, the voice will matter as much as the facts.

4. Add dates and places — even rough ones

“Around 1950” or “a village near Lahore” is far better than nothing. Places let you draw your family’s migration — and most families are surprised by how far they have travelled.

5. Add photos, stories and voices

A list of names is a tree; a photo and a story make it a family. Scan old photos with your phone, write two or three lines about each person, and save a short voice note from the elders while you can.

6. Share it and let others fill the gaps

Send the tree to the family WhatsApp group. Cousins will add names you never knew, and someone always has the photo you were missing. A family tree is never finished — it grows every time someone opens it.
